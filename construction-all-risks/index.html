Construction All Risks

What does Builder's Risk Insurance cover?

A builder's risk policy typically covers damage caused by fire, lightning and explosion; theft or vandalism at the job site; and wind or other accidental events during the construction period.

It's a temporary policy that safeguards the value of your project until construction is complete and the property is ready for occupancy.

What's covered

  • Fire, lightning, explosion
  • Theft or vandalism at the job site
  • Wind and other accidental events during construction

Know the limits

⚠️ What's not covered

Standard builder's risk policies usually exclude earthquakes and floods (unless specifically added as endorsements), damage from wind in coastal or high-risk zones, and workplace injuries or accidents.

You can easily add earthquake or flood riders to your policy — these are often quite affordable and highly recommended depending on your project's location.

Ask About Riders

Standard exclusions

  • Earthquakes & floods (unless endorsed)
  • Wind damage in coastal or high-risk zones
  • Workplace injuries or accidents

Coverage timeline

When should coverage begin and end?

Your construction coverage should start before materials are delivered to the site, ensuring full protection during every stage of the build.

Coverage Starts

Before materials arrive

Coverage begins before materials are delivered to the site, so your project is protected from day one.

Coverage Ends

At completion or transfer

Coverage typically ends upon completion or occupancy of the property, sale or transfer to the new owner, or the policy expiration date. Once the building is complete, switch to a homeowner's or commercial property insurance policy for ongoing protection.
